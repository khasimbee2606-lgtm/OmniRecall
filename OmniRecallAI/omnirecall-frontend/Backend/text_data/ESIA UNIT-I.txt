uniti environmental impact assessment eia is a systematic process to identify predict evaluate and mitigate the environmental social and economic effects of proposed projects before decisions are made environment impact assessment can also be defined as the study to predict the effect of a proposed activityproject on the environment a decisionmaking tool eia compares various alternatives for a project and seeks to identify the one which represents the best combination of economic and environmental costs and benefits history of eia 1969  beginning of eia  eia originated in the united states  the national environmental policy act nepa 1969 made eia mandatory for major federal projects  nepa came into force in 1970 1970s1980s  many countries adopted eia procedures  international organizations such as the world bank began requiring environmental assessments for funded projects eia in india 197879  environmental assessment was first introduced for river valley projects 1986  the environment protection act 1986 was enacted after the bhopal gas tragedy 1994  india issued its first eia notification under the environment protection act  environmental clearance became mandatory for several development projects 2006  a revised eia notification 2006 was issued by the ministry of environment forest and climate change  moefcc  projects were classified into category a and category b for environmental clearance 2020 the draft eia notification 2020 was introduced to replace the eia notification 2006 it proposed changes such as postfacto environmental clearance reduced public consultation time annual compliance reporting and exemptions for certain projects however it received criticism due to concerns about weakening environmental safeguards basic elements of eia  stages of eia the environment impact assessment consists of eight steps with each step equally important in determining the overall performance of the project  screening first stage of eia which determines whether the proposed project requires an eia and if it does then the level of assessment required  scoping this stage identifies the key issues and impacts that should be further investigated this stage also defines the boundary and time limit of the study  impact analysis this stage of eia identifies and predicts the likely environmental and social impact of the proposed project and evaluates the significance  mitigation this step in eia recommends the actions to reduce and avoid the potential adverse environmental consequences of development activities  reporting this stage presents the result of eia in a form of a report to the decisionmaking body and other interested parties  review of eia it examines the adequacy and effectiveness of the eia report and provides the information necessary for decisionmaking  decisionmaking it decides whether the project is rejected approved or needs further change  post monitoring this stage comes into play once the project is commissioned it checks to ensure that the impacts of the project do not exceed the legal standards and implementation of the mitigation measures are in the manner as described in the eia report factors affecting eia factor impact on eia 1 project type  size largercomplex projects need detailed eia 2 location sensitivity ecologically sensitive areas require stricter assessment 3 regulatory requirements eia notification 2006 india mandates categories 4 data availability quality of baseline data affects accuracy 5 stakeholder involvement public participation influences outcomes hedonic pricing method  replacement cost method 1costbenefit analysis cba costbenefit analysis cba is an important economic tool used in the environmental impact assessment eia process to evaluate whether a proposed project is beneficial when compared to its environmental social and economic costs it helps decision makers determine whether a project should be approved modified or rejected in eia costbenefit analysis not only considers financial profits but also includes environmental damages and social impacts caused by the project basic principle a project is considered acceptable if total benefits  total costs if costs exceed benefits the project may not be environmentally and economically suitable objectives of cba in eia 1 to evaluate environmental impacts economically 2 to compare project alternatives 3 to support environmentally sound decisionmaking 4 to identify economically efficient mitigation measures 5 to promote sustainable development role of costbenefit analysis in eia costbenefit analysis plays a major role in environmental impact assessment because it converts environmental effects into monetary values making comparison easier in eia cba helps to  assess environmental damages  evaluate pollution control measures  compare environmental losses and economic gains  select the best project alternative advantages of costbenefit analysis in eia 1 helps in environmental decisionmaking 2 includes environmental impacts in economic terms 3 supports sustainable development 4 helps compare project alternatives 5 encourages efficient use of natural resources limitations of costbenefit analysis in eia 1 difficult to value biodiversity and ecosystem services 2 longterm environmental impacts are uncertain 3 social and cultural values cannot always estimates recreational value based on visitors travel expenses  example value of a national park determined by tourists travel costs 5 hedonic pricing method  uses differences in property prices or willing to pay for environmental improvements  example willingness to pay for cleaning a polluted river environmental impact statement eis environmental impact statement eis is an important document
